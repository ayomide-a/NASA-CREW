A FIELD GUIDE TO LUNAR SITE PLANNING

Every landing site has a sky story.✧

Explore how location, terrain, and time shape the view of the Sun and Earth from the Moon.

01 · THE PLACE

☾Why landing sites matter

A lunar landing site is more than a point on a map. Mission teams weigh science goals alongside slope, surface hazards, lighting, thermal conditions, access to resources, and communications.

Those factors vary across the Moon. Comparing candidate locations helps make trade-offs visible early in mission planning.

02 · THE SKY

☽Why ephemeris matters

An ephemeris predicts where celestial objects appear in the sky at a particular place and time. Here, Sun and Earth azimuth and elevation describe their direction above the local horizon.

Sunlight affects illumination and solar-power opportunities; Earth’s visibility matters for direct-to-Earth communications. Nearby terrain can block either line of sight.

03 · THE TOOL

☾How this explorer works

Choose a site on the 2D map or 3D globe, select a point of interest, or enter coordinates. Set an observation window and request Sun and Earth positions from NASA JPL Horizons.

Review sky-position charts, time above the terrain horizon, and available LOLA topography. Enable comparison to inspect a second site, then explore the results table or download a CSV.

04. THE METHODOLOGY

☽How the landing-site assessment is constructed

The assessment combines NASA JPL Horizons ephemeris with local terrain context from LOLA topography. For each candidate site and time window, we calculate sunlight availability, direct-to-Earth visibility, and terrain-aware line-of-sight conditions, then convert those into weighted objective scores.

These four conditions were selected because they capture the minimum mission-critical viability checks: safe local terrain, usable solar geometry, sustained sunlight for power/thermal stability, and reliable Earth line-of-sight for communications.

Scores are designed for rapid comparison between Site A and Site B during early mission planning. They are decision-support metrics, not a full landing certification.

This explorer supports comparison and mission planning; it is not a complete landing-site safety certification.
